# The Thinking Behind Quorum
*What happens when you bring a decision here, why it's built this way, and the questions people usually ask.*

---

## What Quorum is

Quorum is a place to bring a real decision — a job offer, a relationship, a move, a bet on yourself — and get a structured, honest read on it before you commit. Instead of one AI giving you one opinion, Quorum runs your decision past six advisors who are deliberately built to disagree with each other, asks you the follow-up questions a good friend would ask if they weren't afraid of upsetting you, and then remembers the whole thing — so the tenth decision you bring here is informed by the first nine.

The name is the point: a *quorum* is the minimum number of people who have to be in the room before a decision counts. Nothing here gets rubber-stamped by a single voice — including yours, and including any one advisor.

---

## Why this approach, not just "ask an AI"

A few well-established ideas about how people actually make decisions badly, and what's known to help:

- **People are bad at steelmanning the option they don't want to pick.** Decades of research on judgment and decision-making — going back to Kahneman and Tversky's work on heuristics and cognitive bias — shows that once someone leans toward a choice, they start unconsciously filtering evidence to support it. Structured, deliberately adversarial review — someone whose whole job is to argue the other side — is one of the more reliable known correctives.
- **"Premortems" work.** Imagining a decision has already failed, and working backward to why, surfaces risks that forward-looking optimism tends to hide. That's effectively what a couple of the advisors are doing on every session.
- **Calibration is a trainable skill, not a fixed trait.** Forecasting research — notably Philip Tetlock's work on what separates accurate forecasters from everyone else — shows that people who track their confidence against outcomes over time get measurably better at knowing what they actually know. Quorum's confidence-then-outcome loop is built directly on this: it's why the product asks you to rate your confidence *before* you know how things turn out, and follows up later.
- **Patterns are invisible from inside a single decision.** You can't notice a recurring blind spot from one conversation — you need a record. That's the entire reason Quorum keeps a private history instead of treating every session as disposable.

None of this means Quorum has run its own clinical trials — it's a product built on established principles from decision science, not a substitute for one. We'd rather be honest about that than oversell it.

## What actually happens when you bring a decision

1. **You describe it, in your own words.** No forms, no forced categories.
2. **Six advisors respond, one after another**, each with a fixed, different mandate — one argues against your instinct with real rigor, one maps the worst realistic downside, one finds the pattern this rhymes with (in your own past decisions, and in how situations shaped like this one usually play out), one maps who this actually affects and how they'll really react, one takes the long view, one thinks like your smartest adversary. You can start reading the first response while later ones are still coming in.
3. **You get asked two or three sharp questions, every time** — built around what's emotionally at stake, not generic journaling prompts.
4. **You get a single clear verdict and a named tension** — not just six opinions dumped on you to sort out yourself — along with a quick visual of which advisors weighed most heavily on this particular decision, so it's clear why the verdict leans the way it does.
5. **You can push back on any advisor**, and they'll respond to your specific objection, not repeat themselves.
6. **Later, you log what actually happened.** This step is what turns Quorum from a smart conversation into a system that learns your patterns — it only works if you close the loop.

## What builds up over time

The more decisions you bring, the more Quorum can tell you things no single session could:

- **Your bias fingerprint** — which of a set of well-documented cognitive biases show up in your decisions, and, more usefully, under what specific conditions they actually correlate with worse outcomes for *you* — not a generic "you're prone to X."
- **Your calibration** — whether you tend to be overconfident, underconfident, or accurate, and where.
- **Contradictions** — moments where you stated a principle in one decision and did the opposite in another.
- **A map of your decisions** — visually connecting today's decision to past ones that share a structure, a bias, or an outcome pattern.

## Your privacy

- Anything you personally write — your decision, your context, your answers, your reflections — is encrypted before it's ever stored. Nobody at Quorum reads your decisions as part of normal operation.
- You can start completely anonymously. No email is required to use the core product.
- You can export everything you've ever entered, or delete your account and everything tied to it, at any time.
- If Quorum ever offers any team, workplace, or aggregate benchmarking features, individual decisions are never visible to anyone else — including administrators. Any cross-person insight only ever comes from aggregated patterns across a genuinely large group, never from a single person's data.

---

## What Quorum is not

- **Not therapy, and not a mental health service.** If you're in real distress, please talk to a person — a therapist, a doctor, or a crisis line, not an app.
- **Not legal, financial, or medical advice.** The Council can help you think something through; it can't replace a professional who's actually licensed to advise you on it.
- **Not a replacement for the people in your life.** Quorum is built to sharpen your own judgment, not to become the only voice you check in with.

## FAQ

**Is this just ChatGPT with extra steps?**
No — the whole point is structure. A general chatbot will tell you what you want to hear if you're not careful how you ask. Quorum's six advisors have fixed, non-negotiable jobs specifically so you can't accidentally get six versions of agreement, and the system remembers your decision history in a structured way a normal chat thread doesn't.

**Do I have to sign up to try it?**
No. You can bring a decision to the Council with no account at all. Signing up (just an email, no password required) lets your history follow you across devices and unlocks the longitudinal features once you have enough sessions.

**What if I don't agree with what the Council says?**
Push back. Every advisor will respond directly to your specific objection. Quorum is also built to track how much you push back and whether you revise your stated position afterward — because independent thinking, not compliance, is the goal.

**Do I have to answer the follow-up questions?**
No — you can skip them. Answering them makes the analysis sharper and improves your long-term profile, but it's never required.

**What happens if I don't log an outcome?**
Nothing punitive — but the pattern-recognition features (bias fingerprint, calibration, contradictions) depend on outcomes to work, so the more you log, the more useful Quorum gets over time.

**How much does it cost?**
The core Council experience and your Decision Brief PDF are free. A paid subscription unlocks the longitudinal Mirror layer — your bias fingerprint, calibration tracking, contradiction detection, and your full decision map.

**Can I delete everything?**
Yes, at any time, from your account settings — this permanently removes your sessions, messages, outcomes, and derived profile data.
