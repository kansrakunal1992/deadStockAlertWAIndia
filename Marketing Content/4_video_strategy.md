# Quorum — Video Strategy
*Three videos, fully scripted and ready to hand to a producer or an AI video generation tool — plus exactly where each one lives, on the website and inside the product. **Bootstrapped note:** the scene-by-scene prompts below double as build specs for a genuinely free code-based pipeline — see "The truly free production stack" section before reaching for a paid AI video tool.*

---

## The strategy in one paragraph

Three videos, three different jobs, three different emotional registers — and none of them should try to do more than one job. The **intro video** sells the feeling (you don't have to decide alone, and you don't have to decide badly). The **research video** sells the credibility (this isn't vibes, it's built on real decision science). The **explainer video** sells the mechanism (here's literally what happens when you use it). Trying to cram all three into one video is why most product videos feel bloated and forgettable — keep them separate, keep them short, and place each one exactly where the visitor's question at that moment matches the video's job.

---

## Placement matrix — what goes where

| Video | Website placement | Product placement | Recommended length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Intro / Self-Help** | Home page hero, above the fold, autoplay-muted with captions | First-run screen, before the input box, skippable | 45–60 sec |
| **2. Research-Used** | "The Science" page, embedded after the opening paragraph | Optional link from the weighting strip / Mirror ("Why does Quorum work this way?") | 75–90 sec |
| **3. Explainer** | "How It Works" page hero | Two cuts — a 45 sec teaser inside onboarding, and a longer 2 min version linked from account settings / help center for users who want the full walkthrough | 45 sec (site + onboarding) / 2 min (full cut) |

**Why this length discipline matters:** a website visitor gives a hero video roughly 5–10 seconds before deciding to keep watching or bounce — so every video's first 3 seconds has to earn the rest. A 2+ minute video only belongs somewhere the visitor already opted in (help center, onboarding continuation), never as a hero. Where "lengthy" is genuinely necessary — the full explainer, for people who want the mechanism, not just the pitch — it's placed *after* someone has already shown intent, not in front of them.

## Video 1 — Intro / Self-Help

**Job:** make someone feel *seen* in the specific discomfort of a hard decision, and plant the idea that there's a better way to sit with it than alone, at 1am, re-reading the same three texts.

**Tone:** quiet, honest, a little intimate — not corporate, not hype. Think: the voice of someone who's genuinely been there, not a brand.

**Target length:** 45–60 seconds.

### Narrative / shot list

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Dark, quiet frame. A single soft light. Abstract — not a literal person's face, just a suggestion of stillness, maybe a hand hovering over a phone screen, out of focus. | *"There's a specific kind of quiet that happens right before a big decision."* |
| 0:06–0:14 | Quick, restless cuts — a clock, a half-written text message being deleted, a scroll of old conversations, pacing feet. Slightly faster pace, subtle tension in the score. | *"You've run it in your head fifty times. Asked the same three people. Still don't trust your own read on it."* |
| 0:14–0:22 | The visual calms. A single warm light appears — six soft points of light arranging themselves around it, like chairs settling around a table. | *"Quorum isn't one more opinion shouting into the noise."* |
| 0:22–0:34 | The six points of light each "speak" briefly — shown as gentle pulses of light with a few key words appearing and fading: "the other side." "the downside." "the pattern." "who benefits." "ten years from now." "what they'd want you to miss." | *"It's six distinct ways of thinking, built to disagree with each other — so you get the fight you'd never have with yourself."* |
| 0:34–0:44 | The six lights resolve into one clear line of light — a single, calm, legible verdict. Warm, resolved color palette. | *"And it remembers. So the next hard decision isn't a blank page — it's the twelfth conversation, not the first."* |
| 0:44–0:52 | Wordmark: **QUORUM**. Tagline beneath, simple. Soft fade. | *"Quorum. Bring the decision. You won't sit with it alone."* |
| 0:52–0:58 | End card: CTA — "Try it free, no signup." | (no VO — silence, or a single soft musical resolve) |

### AI video generation prompt (for Sora / Runway / Kling / Pika-style tools)

```
STYLE: Cinematic, minimal, abstract — no literal human faces. Warm-to-cool color 
grade shifting from cold blue/grey tension to warm amber resolution. Soft depth 
of field, gentle camera drift, no hard cuts on beats — slow dissolves.

DURATION: 45–55 seconds, 6 scenes.

SCENE 1 (0-6s): Extreme close, shallow focus. A single soft point of warm light 
in near-total darkness. Slow, almost imperceptible push-in. Mood: stillness, 
quiet weight.

SCENE 2 (6-14s): Quick montage, handheld/restless energy. Fragments only, never 
a full face: a clock's second hand, a phone screen with a message being typed 
and deleted, a blurred pacing silhouette. Cool color temperature. Mood: 
restlessness, repetition, being stuck in your own head.

SCENE 3 (14-22s): Hard cut to stillness. Six small points of soft light drift 
in from the edges of frame and arrange themselves in a loose circle around the 
original light, like chairs settling around a table. Color warms slightly. 
Mood: arrival, structure appearing out of chaos.

SCENE 4 (22-34s): The six lights each pulse gently in turn, one at a time, 
each accompanied by a single short phrase appearing in clean minimal 
typography and fading before the next: "the other side" / "the downside" / 
"the pattern" / "who benefits" / "ten years from now" / "what they'd want you 
to miss." Rhythmic, deliberate pacing — not frantic.

SCENE 5 (34-44s): The six lights draw inward and resolve into a single steady 
horizontal line of warm light, clean and legible, center frame. Color palette 
fully warm now. Mood: resolution, clarity, calm confidence.

SCENE 6 (44-58s): Slow fade to a clean wordmark card: "QUORUM" in minimal 
serif or geometric sans, centered, on a warm dark background, with a short 
tagline beneath and a simple CTA. 

NO on-screen human faces. NO literal chairs/boardroom imagery — keep it 
abstract/light-based throughout. NO text-heavy scenes — max one short phrase 
on screen at a time. Music: sparse, single instrument (piano or soft strings), 
building from tension to a resolved, unhurried final chord.
```

## Video 2 — Research-Used

**Job:** answer, credibly and quickly, "why should I trust this / is this real science or just AI marketing." This is the video for the skeptical, thoughtful visitor — not the emotional one.

**Tone:** confident, a little more information-dense than Video 1, but still visual and paced — not a lecture, not talking-head narration over a stock deck.

**Target length:** 75–90 seconds.

### Narrative / shot list

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | A single figure (abstract silhouette, not literal) standing at a fork — two paths diverging, visualized simply, like a branching line diagram. | *"Left to itself, your brain doesn't evaluate both paths fairly. It picks a favorite fast — and then just collects reasons to agree with itself."* |
| 0:08–0:20 | The branching line diagram: one path glows brighter almost instantly, while small "supporting" ticks accumulate only on that path, ignoring the other. A small label appears: "confirmation bias" — brief, not clinical. | *"That's not a flaw unique to you. It's one of the most well-documented patterns in how people actually decide — and it's exactly what a genuinely adversarial second opinion is built to interrupt."* |
| 0:20–0:34 | Cut to a simple visual: a decision "failing" in fast-forward, then rewinding, with a small magnifying glass tracing back through the failure to its root cause, before it's actually made. | *"Imagining a decision has already gone wrong, and working backward from there, surfaces risks your optimism quietly filters out. It's called a premortem — and it's baked into how Quorum's advisors are built to think."* |
| 0:34–0:50 | A simple dial/gauge visual — a needle oscillating between "overconfident" and "underconfident," slowly narrowing toward center over several repeated cycles. | *"And confidence itself is trainable. People who track what they predicted against what actually happened get measurably better at knowing what they really know — it's the core skill behind the best professional forecasters."* |
| 0:50–1:04 | A single point of light on a blank field. Then a second point appears nearby, connected by a thin line. Then several more — a small constellation forming, clearly patterned, not random. | *"None of this is visible from inside one decision. It only shows up as a record — which is the entire reason Quorum remembers."* |
| 1:04–1:18 | Return to calm wordmark-adjacent visual. Simple on-screen text: "Built on established decision science. Not clinically studied as a product — and we'll always tell you that plainly." | *"We didn't invent these ideas. We just built a place that actually uses them, every time — and we'd rather be honest about what we know and don't know than oversell it to you."* |
| 1:18–1:26 | End card, CTA to "The Science" page for more depth. | (soft musical resolve, no VO) |

### AI video generation prompt

```
STYLE: Clean, editorial, diagram-driven motion graphics. Think a sophisticated 
explainer for a serious publication — not corporate stock-footage energy, not 
cartoonish. Muted, confident palette: charcoal, warm white, single accent 
color (amber or deep teal) used sparingly for emphasis only.

DURATION: 75-90 seconds, 6 scenes, driven by simple animated line/node 
diagrams rather than live footage or literal human figures.

SCENE 1 (0-8s): A single abstract silhouette (line-art, non-literal) stands 
at the base of a branching path diagram — two lines diverging upward and 
outward. Camera static, minimal movement.

SCENE 2 (8-20s): One branch of the path animates brighter/thicker rapidly; 
small tick marks accumulate along only that branch while the other dims and 
is neglected. A small, understated text label fades in: "confirmation bias." 
Keep label typography small and non-intrusive — this is not a textbook.

SCENE 3 (20-34s): Cut to a simple timeline visual: an icon representing "a 
decision" moves forward, hits a red failure marker, then the timeline visibly 
rewinds with a magnifying-glass cursor tracing backward to identify the 
root cause before the decision point. Smooth, deliberate motion.

SCENE 4 (34-50s): A minimal semicircular gauge/dial with a needle. The needle 
oscillates in decreasing arcs between two labeled poles ("overconfident" / 
"underconfident"), gradually settling nearer center over 3-4 visible cycles. 
Accent color highlights the needle only.

SCENE 5 (50-64s): Blank dark field. A single point of light appears, then a 
second connects to it with a thin line, then several more join in a clearly 
non-random constellation pattern — echo the visual language of scene from 
Video 1's "six lights" for brand consistency.

SCENE 6 (64-78s): Calm resolution — wordmark-adjacent card with restrained 
on-screen text (max 2 short lines) and a CTA. 

NO literal talking-head narration shots. NO stock-footage business-meeting 
imagery. Keep every diagram simple enough to understand at a glance with 
sound off. Music: understated, editorial — think a measured piano/strings 
bed, not tension-building, more "confident and clear."
```

## Video 3 — Explainer (How Quorum Helps)

**Job:** show, mechanically and quickly, what actually happens end to end. This is the video for someone who's already interested and wants to know "OK, but what do I actually *do*."

**Tone:** brisk, clear, almost demo-like — closer to a well-made product walkthrough than either of the other two. Confidence through clarity, not emotion.

**Target length:** 45 sec cut for the website hero and onboarding; optional extended 2 min cut for the in-product help center / account settings.

### Narrative / shot list (45-second cut)

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | A clean text input field, cursor blinking. A single line of text types itself in: "Should I take the offer?" | *"You bring a real decision. In your own words."* |
| 0:05–0:16 | Six small avatars/icons (abstract, distinct silhouettes or simple icon marks — not cartoon characters) appear one at a time, left to right, each with a one-word label beneath as it "speaks": Contrarian → Risk Architect → Pattern Analyst → Stakeholder Mirror → Elder → Competitor. Each pulses briefly as its label appears. | *"Six advisors respond — one after another, each built to look at it completely differently."* |
| 0:16–0:23 | A short question card appears, simple, with a small icon (a question mark in a soft circle). | *"Then a couple of sharp follow-up questions — every time, tailored to what's actually at stake."* |
| 0:23–0:32 | The six icons collapse into a single verdict card with a headline sentence, and beneath it, a small horizontal bar chart showing 2-3 of the six advisors weighted higher than the rest. | *"And a clear verdict — plus a look at exactly which advisors weighed in most on your specific decision."* |
| 0:32–0:40 | A small calendar/checkmark icon appears — "log what happened" — then a zoom-out to a small node-and-line graph forming from several past sessions. | *"Later, you log what actually happened — and Quorum starts connecting the dots across everything you've brought it."* |
| 0:40–0:45 | Wordmark + CTA: "Bring your first decision — free." | (soft resolve) |

### Extended cut (2 min) — additional beats to insert for the in-product / help-center version

Insert after the 0:32 verdict beat, before the "log what happened" beat:

- **+15s:** Brief beat on pushing back on an advisor — a message bubble with a counter-argument, and the advisor icon responding directly to it (not repeating itself). VO: *"Disagree with an advisor? Push back — they'll respond to your actual objection, not recycle the same answer."*
- **+20s:** Brief beat on the Decision Brief PDF — a document icon assembling itself from the six advisor sections plus a synthesis page, then a small "download" motion. VO: *"Every session becomes a clean, exportable brief — yours to keep, no paywall."*
- **+20s:** Brief beat on the Mirror layer over time — the node-graph visual expanding further, with small labeled clusters appearing ("patterns," "calibration," "contradictions"). VO: *"And the more decisions you bring, the more Quorum can show you about how you actually decide — patterns you'd never catch from inside just one conversation."*

## Cross-video consistency notes

- **Shared visual language:** the "six points of light" motif from Video 1, the "constellation forming" motif from Video 2, and the "node-and-edge graph" from Video 3 should all read as the same visual idea at increasing levels of concreteness — abstract feeling → credibility diagram → literal product UI. This repetition across all three videos is what makes them feel like one coherent brand system rather than three disconnected pieces, even though they live on different pages.
- **No literal human faces or named public figures** in any of the three — keeps things universal, avoids any casting/likeness complications, and keeps the visual system fully ownable and reusable as the product evolves.
- **Captions burned in or supported everywhere** — most website visitors watch muted; if the video only works with sound, it won't land as a hero.
- **Voice:** one consistent VO artist (or a consistent voice profile if synthetic) across all three, so the brand voice doesn't shift between pages.

## Beyond these three (optional, later)

Not requested, but worth flagging as natural next additions once the core three exist and there's real usage data to draw on:

- **Short (15–20s) social cutdowns** of Video 1's most emotionally resonant beat (Scene 2→3 transition, "you're not alone in this") for paid/organic social — the intro video is the one built to travel best in isolation.
- **A real-usage testimonial video**, once there are genuine users willing to speak on camera — this is the highest-trust format available but shouldn't be attempted with actors or composite/fictionalized quotes; it only works if it's real.
- **A short "what's inside the Mirror" video**, gated behind the paywall itself as an unlock moment, rather than public — this would show (not tell) the fingerprint/calibration/contradiction/graph payoff to someone who's already a few sessions in, as a conversion nudge toward the paid tier.

## The truly free production stack (no credits, no tokens, no watermarks)

Worth being direct about this: almost every "free" AI video generator in 2026 — Kling, Sora, Pika, Runway, HeyGen — hands out free credits, not free forever, and watermarks anything produced under the free tier. None of them qualify as truly free for a bootstrapped launch. The reframe that makes "truly free" realistic here: the style scripted above — abstract light points, node graphs, clean bar charts, minimal typography, no photorealism, no human faces — is exactly the kind of thing code-based motion graphics handles better anyway. Exact colors, exact timing, perfectly legible text, zero random-generation artifacts, and fully re-editable forever. None of the three videos above need an AI video generator.

**1. Build the animations — Remotion (free).** Remotion renders video from React/CSS code. Its license is free for individuals and companies up to three people, unlimited videos, commercial use allowed — a two-person team fits comfortably under that. Feed it the shot-by-shot breakdowns above directly: each scene's timing, colors, and on-screen text become components, so what comes out matches the script exactly rather than an AI's interpretation of it. As of January 2026, Remotion also ships an Agent Skill for Claude Code — you can hand Claude Code the scene descriptions above in plain English and have it scaffold the actual components, which is worth trying given Quorum's codebase is already built with Claude Code.

**2. Or skip the framework — HTML/CSS/SVG + free capture.** If a React pipeline feels like overkill for three short videos, build each scene as a plain HTML/CSS/SVG animation instead (I can generate this directly, no separate tool needed) and turn it into an MP4 at zero cost with:
- **Puppeteer or Playwright** (free, open source) — headlessly records the animation frame-by-frame, or
- **OBS Studio** (free, open source, no watermark, no time limit) — screen-records the animation playing in a browser.

**3. Voiceover — Kokoro or Piper, not ElevenLabs' free tier.** Kokoro (Apache 2.0) and Piper (MIT) are open-weight, run on an ordinary CPU (no GPU needed), sound natural enough for this kind of VO, and their licenses explicitly permit commercial use with no per-character or per-minute cost, ever. Skip Coqui/XTTS for this specific use — excellent quality, but its voice-cloning weights carry a non-commercial license.

**4. Music — Pixabay Music or YouTube Audio Library.** Both are genuinely free and commercially safe (Pixabay needs no attribution; check individual YouTube Audio Library tracks, some want a credit line). A single sparse piano or strings bed, as scripted above, is easy to find in either.

**5. Assembly, captions, final export — DaVinci Resolve (free).** The free version is a complete, non-watermarked, no-time-limit editor — timeline editing, color, Fairlight audio, and Fusion compositing all included. This is where the Remotion (or screen-recorded) visuals get synced with the Kokoro/Piper voiceover, the music bed goes in, and captions get burned in.

**If literal AI-generated (photorealistic) footage is ever wanted later** — worth flagging honestly: the only genuinely free (not freemium) way to generate that kind of footage is self-hosting an open-weight model like Wan 2.2 or LTX-Video, which is free forever once downloaded, no credits, no watermark — but it needs a real GPU (8GB+ VRAM minimum). Without one already in hand, "free" quietly becomes a hardware cost. Google Colab and Kaggle both still offer genuinely free (no-payment) GPU hours for occasional experimentation, capped by session length rather than a paywall — fine for testing, not a reliable production pipeline. Given the style of these three videos, this route probably isn't needed at all.
