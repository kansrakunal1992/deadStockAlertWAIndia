**C-01** · Family business owner · Western
I'm deciding whether to sell our century-old family food business to a large conglomerate for a substantial sum, or to keep running it as a family enterprise. The business is financially healthy — there's no pressure to sell. But our family's shared identity, annual rituals, and generational pride are all tied to ownership. I'm not sure money can replace what we'd be giving up.

---

**C-02** · Family business successor · Western
My family needs to decide how to structure my transition into running our large construction business. My parent fears that mentoring me directly could damage our relationship. I haven't had real operating authority yet. The options are direct mentorship, sending me for external development, or a structured handover — but we haven't agreed on which path protects both the business and the family.

---

**C-03** · Founder's daughter · Western
I've been given more and more responsibility in my father's business, but with no clarity about my future role or authority. The ambiguity is affecting our relationship — I want a father, not a manager. I'm deciding whether to stay and try to navigate this, or to step away, build my credentials elsewhere, and return on clearer terms.

---

**C-04** · Founder · Family succession · Western
I've avoided explicitly deciding who will control my business after I'm gone, and how my children — some active in the business, some not — will be treated. I'm deciding whether to have those conversations now, while I can shape the outcome, or to leave things unspecified and let the family sort it out later.

---

**C-05** · Wealthy heiress · European
I've inherited a very large fortune that I don't believe I earned. Keeping it feels morally wrong to me. I'm deciding whether to hold the inheritance — preserving my security — or give away the vast majority of it and publicly campaign for policies that would tax wealth like mine. There's no external deadline. This is entirely a question of what I believe is right.

---

**C-06** · Wealthy heiress · Western
I was raised inside a family system built to compound wealth across generations. I've come to believe that extreme wealth concentration causes real harm. I'm deciding whether to give away a significant portion of my inheritance and publicly advocate against policies that benefit people like me — or to preserve the security and social position my family built.

---

**C-07** · Heiress and impact investor · Western
I've received a very large inheritance and I'm deciding what to do with my life and capital. I could continue living conventionally — maintaining my career in the arts — or I could redirect everything toward philanthropy and impact investing. The second path would mean leaving my career, committing substantial capital to a foundation, and building an entirely new identity from scratch.

---

**C-08** · Heir · Multigenerational wealthy family · Western
I've been drifting — experiencing professional anxiety, comparing myself to my high-achieving parent, and using substances to cope with the pressure. I'm deciding whether to continue inside the logic of inherited wealth and status, or to commit to sobriety and reorient my life around purpose and service rather than prestige. I don't know who I am if I'm not defined by this family.

---

**C-09** · First-generation immigrant founder · Asian
I'm from mainland China and couldn't get a US visa. I'm deciding whether to stay and build a life at home, or to emigrate — first to Germany for engineering studies, then potentially to the US if family sponsorship comes through. Leaving means abandoning familiar networks, accepting real career uncertainty, and rebuilding my identity somewhere I don't belong yet.

---

**C-10** · Startup founder/CEO · Western
I've been running my venture-backed company through sustained burnout — chronic stress, health deteriorating in ways not visible externally. I'm deciding whether to keep going as CEO, or to step down from day-to-day operations to recover while staying involved in a non-executive capacity. The company is at an important growth stage and stepping down feels like abandoning something I built.

---

**C-11** · Technology entrepreneur · Western
My aerospace company has failed three launches in a row. I have capital for one final attempt — and no more. I must decide within days whether to commit everything to a fourth launch, knowing failure ends the company permanently. At the same time, a separate major business I control is in financial crisis. Both are at risk simultaneously.

---

**C-12** · Entrepreneur · Western
I left a company I helped build to start a new venture in the same industry. Now I've learned the original company's founders want to sell — and I have a window of weeks to raise acquisition capital before competing buyers move in. Acquiring it would combine my new venture with the original brand and infrastructure. Missing this window means losing the opportunity permanently.

---

**C-13** · Technology entrepreneur · South Asian
Late one evening I received news of a major government economic policy change. Overnight it will create massive demand for cashless payment infrastructure — exactly what my company does. I have roughly 72 hours to decide how aggressively to scale servers, marketing, and operations. Over-scaling risks burning cash on demand that may not last. Under-scaling means losing a once-in-a-generation market moment.

---

**C-14** · Entrepreneur · South Asian
I'm 25 years old with a brewing science background. A foreign partner has offered to co-found a biotechnology company in my home country — a sector that doesn't exist here yet. The partner leaves within days. I have no lab, no capital, no team, and no precedent to follow. I have to decide now whether to accept.

---

**C-15** · Retail entrepreneur · Western
I spent five years building a franchise store into the most profitable in the regional network. I've just discovered my lease has no renewal clause — the landlord is reclaiming the property for a family member. I must vacate within weeks, losing everything I built. I'm deciding between three paths: give up retail entirely, fight the landlord legally, or start over somewhere new with a different concept.

---

**C-16** · Entrepreneur couple · Western
I want to leave my career at a successful financial firm, relocate our family across the country, and start a business in a nascent technology sector. My partner has an established writing career in our current city. We genuinely need to align — not just agree on paper — on leaving financial security and relocating, with no guarantee the new venture works.

---

**C-17** · Founder couple · Western
I've built a consumer brand over years and now need capital to compete globally. The most viable path is an IPO. I value independence and fear public market accountability will compromise the culture I've built. My partner and key advisors support the IPO. We need to reach a real decision — one that changes the business and my role in it permanently.

---

**C-18** · Founder couple · Western
My partner and I have decided we want to give our company away rather than sell it or leave it to our children. We agree on the direction. But we disagree on the governance structure, how to protect the culture, and which organisations should receive it. We need to agree on a permanent, irrevocable structure — one we cannot undo once it's done.

---

**C-19** · Technology entrepreneur couple · South Asian
My partner and I have committed to giving away the majority of our wealth. We disagree on how. I believe in systemic, technology-enabled interventions at scale. My partner believes in grassroots, community-led approaches. We need to genuinely align — not just compromise — on how to deploy substantial philanthropic capital in a way we can both stand behind.

---

**C-20** · Entrepreneur couple · Western
I want to launch an airline to compete directly with dominant carriers on a major international route. My partner and board strongly oppose the move. I believe the downside is bounded by a lease structure with an exit clause. We need to reach a genuine decision — not a provisional one — on whether to proceed. My partner's opposition is real, not just caution.

---

**C-21** · Technology company CEO · Western
I'm the CEO of a major semiconductor company. Our founding product line has been made structurally unviable by international competition. I'm deciding whether to exit it entirely — redirecting all resources to our secondary product category. That means large-scale layoffs and dismantling significant institutional knowledge built over decades. There is no clean version of this decision.

---

**C-22** · Media company CEO · Western
I'm in my first year as CEO and considering acquiring a leading animation studio for $7.4 billion in stock — while our own animation business is failing. The deal requires negotiating directly with a controlling founder who has historically been hostile to us. If the attempt fails publicly, my tenure almost certainly ends. But not attempting it may mean the company falls further behind.

---

**C-23** · Technology company CEO · South Asian
I've been offered the CEO role at one of the world's largest technology companies. The offer arrived while my son — who has severe cerebral palsy — is hospitalised. My wife has structured our entire family life around his care needs in our current city. Accepting means materially disrupting that structure and exposing our family to intense public scrutiny. I need to decide.

---

**C-24** · Consumer brand CEO · Western
I've returned as CEO to revive a brand that has lost its way. I'm deciding whether to close all retail locations for several hours on our busiest commercial day of the year — at a cost of millions in lost revenue — to retrain every customer-facing employee on our quality standards. Internal and analyst opposition is strong. My rationale is values-driven, not supported by data.

---

**C-25** · Industrial conglomerate chairman · South Asian
I'm considering acquiring two iconic British automotive brands from an American seller — at a loss — at the onset of a global financial crisis, when the automotive market is declining and most of my advisors oppose the deal. I've personally visited the factories. I believe the cultural fit matters as much as the financials. Almost everyone around me thinks this is the wrong moment.
