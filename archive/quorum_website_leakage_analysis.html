Executive summary

The website does the most important thing correctly: it names a category ("Judgment Infrastructure"), frames the right problem (intelligence abundant, coherence scarce), and communicates a transformation narrative that compounds over time. None of that is at risk. The positioning is strong.

However, the current copy makes six material errors that a product-oriented competitor at an equivalent stage could act on directly. In aggregate, the website reveals the ontology cardinality, the rule engine mode-set, the full processing pipeline in sequential order, and six named internal modules alongside their computational mechanisms. A well-funded team reading this knows exactly what to build.

The root cause is consistent: the copy describes how the system works rather than what transformation the user experiences. These are different sentences. Quorum should be writing the second one.

The core diagnostic principle

Three categories of copy.
Different rules for each.

The issue is not which words are used. It is what each sentence exposes. Before publishing any product copy, every sentence should be placed in one of three buckets.

✓ Always publish

"What Quorum does" — what the user experiences, what transformation is possible, what category this occupies. Impossible to reverse-engineer. Strengthens positioning.

⚠ Abstract first

"What the system produces" — outputs, findings, patterns surfaced. Valuable as positioning. Safe if abstracted from mechanism. Risky if mechanism description is attached.

✕ Default to remove

"How Quorum does it" — pipeline sequence, dimension counts, rule counts, mode names, scoring logic, trigger conditions. This is a product specification. Default is off.

Issue 01 Exact dimension count disclosed — with named dimensions listed
High
Appears twice — stats section + Step 00 description
"14 Structural dimensions scored before any advisor responds"
Step 00 body copy
"…the decision is classified across 14 structural dimensions. Reversibility. Identity stakes. Real vs. felt urgency. Prior unresolved dependencies."

The number 14 and four named dimensions together hand a competitor the skeleton of the ontology tagger. They know: it is a multi-dimensional classifier, it scores approximately 14 axes, and at least reversibility, identity stakes, urgency type, and upstream dependency are among them. This is enough to bootstrap a structurally similar system — not just the concept, but the architecture. The number also signals that this is a shipped, calibrated system (not aspirational), which makes it worth reverse-engineering. Removing one or the other would help. Having both together makes it a specification extract.

Rewrite — stat
Replace the stat number with an outcome-oriented claim:

"Structural dimensions read before any response runs" — drop the number, keep the concept. The stat's job is to signal rigor, not disclose cardinality. Alternatively: replace the stat entirely with something that communicates the downstream effect — "Every advisor response is shaped by what the decision structurally is, not just what you wrote."
Rewrite — Step 00 body
Remove the named dimensions. Replace with outcome language:

"Before any advisor runs, your decision has been read at a structural level — what kind of decision this actually is, not just what you wrote. That read is silent. You don't fill in a form. But it changes everything that follows."

The named dimensions (reversibility, identity stakes, etc.) are category-creation-positive in the abstract — they help the user understand the product. But they appear here in a pipeline description, not in a positioning claim, which makes them a spec sheet rather than a promise.
Issue 02 Rule engine count plus all three operating modes published verbatim
High
Stats section
"12 Intervention rules — some decisions get stopped before advice runs"
Examiner section, es-claim block
"Some decisions get stopped entirely — The rule engine can block synthesis, gate it behind one critical question, or mark the Council's output provisional. The mode depends on what the structural analysis finds — not on your framing."

Together these two sentences describe a deterministic rule engine with 12 rules and three modes: block, gate, provisional. That is an architecture specification, not a positioning claim. A competitor now knows exactly what to build: a pre-synthesis classifier with a three-branch decision tree. The number 12 is particularly damaging — it signals that the rule set has been enumerated and calibrated, and it constrains a competitor's implementation target. The three modes (block/gate/provisional) map directly to R1/R7, R2/R3/R10, and R4/R5/R6/R8/R9/R12 from the internal rule engine — a sharp reader with product intuition could reconstruct most of this taxonomy from the website copy alone.

Rewrite — stat
Remove the count entirely. Replace the label with an outcome description:

"Some decisions are held before synthesis runs" — the stat number becomes the count of decisions that have been redirected, or is removed entirely and replaced by a more defensible claim.
Rewrite — three-mode description
The Examiner section already contains the stronger version of this claim — use that. The three-mode mechanical description should be replaced with the transformation the user experiences:

"Not every decision runs all the way through. Sometimes the Examiner finds that a prior question has to be resolved first. Sometimes the framing itself is the problem. In either case, you hear that before the Council's analysis is treated as settled."
Issue 03 Full pipeline architecture published in sequential numbered order
High
"One decision. Five layers." — Steps section
"00 — Silent · automatic: Structural Analysis → 01 — Free: The Council → 02 — Free: The Examiner → 03 — Free: Synthesis → 04 — Mirror · Judgment OS: Mirror"

This is a product architecture diagram rendered as marketing copy. In sequence: pre-processing classification, parallel multi-persona generation, post-persona gating layer, synthesis, persistent memory. The internal naming convention is visible in the step tags ("Silent · automatic" = the ontology tagger; "Free" = Council/Examiner/Synthesis run without paywall; "Mirror · Judgment OS" = the paid layer). The description of Step 02 — "Derived from the structural analysis — not a generic checklist" — explicitly confirms that the Examiner reads the output of Step 00, which describes the integration architecture. A reader with product intuition now knows: build a pre-processing classifier → feed output to six parallel personas → run a second-pass examiner that reads the classifier output → synthesize → store persistently. That is the entire product.

Rewrite — frame as experience, not pipeline
Preserve the five-act structure (it is good UX communication) but rewrite every step description in experience language, not system language:

Step 00 — "Before you hear from a single advisor, your decision has already been read at a structural level. Not what you wrote — what the decision actually is. Silent. Automatic."

Step 01 — "Six advisors review the decision in parallel, each from a distinct analytical frame. Each one's analysis is shaped by what the structural read found."

Step 02 — "Before synthesis, a separate examination runs. Its job is to find the question you haven't asked yet — and sometimes, to tell you the decision isn't ready."

Step 03 — "A single directional position, integrating all six perspectives. Not a summary of views. A call."

Step 04 — "The part that compounds. Every session adds to a calibrated record of how you specifically think. This is the product."

The "Derived from structural analysis" construction in Step 02 is the most dangerous sentence in this section. It explicitly confirms the examiner reads the classifier output — remove this and replace with the outcome.
Issue 04 Six internal module names plus their computational mechanisms in the pricing list
High
Mirror tier feature list — verbatim
"Bias Fingerprint — the exact structural conditions that activate your patterns, not generic labels" · "Confidence Calibration — a running record of where your certainty is reliable and where it consistently overshoots" · "Implicit Rules — the operating principles extracted from how you actually reason, not how you think you do" · "Contradiction Detector — flagged automatically when you violate your own stated principles across decisions" · "Decision Independence Score — how much your calls are driven by your own judgment vs. social pressure"

The names themselves are brand vocabulary and safe to publish. The mechanism descriptions are not. "Operating principles extracted from how you actually reason" describes a rules-extraction pipeline. "Flagged automatically when you violate your own stated principles across decisions" describes a cross-session contradiction detection system. "Where your certainty is reliable and where it consistently overshoots" describes a pre/post confidence calibration model. "How much your calls are driven by your own judgment vs. social pressure" describes a social influence scoring module. A product team reading this has a Mirror feature specification — six modules, each with its primary computation described in plain English. The module names should stay as brand vocabulary. The mechanism sub-descriptions should be replaced with outcome descriptions.

Rewrite — abstract mechanism, keep outcome
"Bias Fingerprint — how and when your patterns activate, specific to your decision history"
"Confidence Calibration — the gap between how confident you were and how right you were"
"Your Implicit Rules — the operating principles your decisions actually reveal"
"Contradiction Detector — when a decision you just made conflicts with a principle from your record"
"What Keeps Coming Up — the structural conditions that recur without resolution"
"Decision Independence Score — how much this decision came from you, not from the room"

Each rewrite preserves what the user needs to understand the value proposition while removing the mechanism description that tells a competitor how to build it.

Issue 05 Bias trigger mechanism exposed via example (conditional activation logic)
Medium
Mirror showcase — Bias Fingerprint section
"FOMO activates when a trusted contact endorses a deal before you've had time to form an independent view." · "First number in a negotiation frame tends to anchor your counter-offer range."

The example is excellent for conversion — it shows the product's precision at a glance. The problem is it reveals the detection architecture. "Activates when [trusted contact] endorses [deal] under [time pressure]" shows that Quorum conditions bias detection on contextual triggers — not just detecting FOMO as a category, but detecting the specific conditions that activate it per decision. A competitor now knows to build conditional bias classification, not categorical bias labeling. This is a meaningful product architecture reveal. The example itself (showing the output) can stay. The trigger-logic framing ("activates when...") should be abstracted.

Rewrite — show output, not trigger logic
Keep the card. Change the framing from trigger-condition to finding:

"FOMO / Social proof — active in 3 decisions over 4 months"
"When a trusted contact endorsed a deal before you'd formed a view of your own."

The finding stays specific (this is what makes it convert). The mechanism — "activates when [condition]" — is abstracted to a description of when it occurred, not a description of how the system detects it.
Issue 06 Decision Independence Score mechanism — pre/post framework-adoption scoring
Medium
Mirror showcase — Decision Independence Score
"Quorum tracks whether you incorporate its frameworks into your own thinking unprompted — before the Council runs. A rising score means your judgment is compounding. A flat score means something isn't landing."

This sentence describes the scoring mechanism precisely: compare the user's framing before the Council runs against Quorum's frameworks, measure internalization over time. A competitor now has a specific module specification: pre-session framing classifier, framework vocabulary comparison, longitudinal delta tracking. This is a nuanced product insight that is not obvious — it was clearly the result of design iteration. Publishing the mechanism gives it away.

Rewrite — outcome only
"Proof the product is actually working. The score is a direct measure of how much your judgment has compounded — and it moves. A rising score means something has changed in how you decide. A flat score is a signal too. You can see both."

The mechanism (what Quorum is comparing, when it runs, how it scores) is removed. What remains is the value proposition and the user experience — which is everything the prospective user needs to know.
Issue 07 Examiner mock uses internal mode language ("Synthesis is held / marked provisional")
Medium
REDIRECT mock card / Examiner section
"Synthesis is held. The Council's analysis is marked provisional. A different question is surfaced first." · "Synthesis is blocked. The Council's analysis is marked provisional."

"Synthesis is held," "marked provisional," and "synthesis is blocked" are internal state descriptions for the REDIRECT and GATE modes of the rule engine. They appear in what reads as UI output but are in fact system state labels. A competitor sees: there is a holding state for synthesis, a provisional state for council output, and these are distinct states. The button label "Reframe the question →" further reveals that REDIRECT specifically triggers a reformulation flow — this is the internal R1/R7 design logic made visible.

Rewrite — translate internal states to user experience
In the mock card:
"The Council has reviewed the decision. Before synthesis runs, there's a prior question that has to be answered first."

Remove "Synthesis is held," "marked provisional," and "blocked" entirely. The mode tag "Not ready" is fine — it describes the user experience, not the system state. The button can stay conceptually but the mock is non-interactive anyway — consider removing the button and letting the text carry the moment.
Issue 08 Social proof section reveals examiner reasoning pattern and bias trigger
Medium
Social proof card — outcome block
"What the Examiner found: The salary decision was structurally premature. The demand forecast — the variable that determined whether fixed raises were an investment or an irreversible cost — was unresolved. Synthesis was blocked."
Social proof metrics strip
"FOMO — Bias surfaced by Mirror — activating specifically under competitive hiring pressure"

The social proof card is otherwise strong. Two specific phrases create leakage. First: "Synthesis was blocked" — same internal state language as Issue 07. Second: "activating specifically under competitive hiring pressure" — this is the conditional trigger pattern from Issue 05 applied to a real case. Together they confirm the mechanism is operational, not aspirational, and show a competitor what a live output looks like from the trigger-detection system.

Change "Synthesis was blocked" → "The decision was held."

Change "activating specifically under competitive hiring pressure" → "Flagged by Mirror during this decision" or simply "Bias flagged during decision" with the specific condition removed.

Everything else in the social proof card should stay — it is one of the strongest sections on the site and converts well. The two phrase changes above remove the mechanism language without weakening the case.

Final note

The goal is not to obscure.
It is to abstract at the right altitude.

None of the rewrites above weaken the positioning. In every case, the rewritten version is stronger as a conversion and category-creation instrument — because it describes the transformation, not the mechanism, and the transformation is what the user needs to believe before they sign up.

The current copy errs toward over-explanation — a common pattern in technically confident founders who want the reader to understand how hard the problem was to solve. That instinct is correct. The execution should shift: let the output carry the evidence of difficulty, not the description of the pipeline.

The website should make the reader feel the inevitability of the product and the precision of the analysis — while making it impossible to reverse-engineer how either is achieved. That gap — between the sense of sophisticated precision and the opacity of how it works — is exactly where premium positioning lives.
