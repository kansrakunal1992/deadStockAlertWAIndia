On dynamic card ordering

Answer to question 2 — should Mirror sections reorder dynamically based on what changed and what suits the decision-maker?

Recommendation: Not full dynamic reordering. An Attention Zone + stable body instead.

Full page reordering is the wrong answer for a judgment infrastructure product. Here is why, and what to do instead.

Why not full dynamic reorder: Users with 50 sessions have built spatial memory of Mirror. They know Contradiction Detector is below Patterns, which is below Independence Score. Destroying that muscle memory on each visit trades short-term "freshness" for long-term disorientation. More importantly, predictability = trustworthiness for an HNI decision tool. A page that rearranges itself reads as a social feed — entertainment logic, not judgment infrastructure logic. The sticky nav (item #3) already gives the user "jump to what I care about" without disrupting the layout they know.

What dynamic ordering should look like — the Attention Zone: Between the Summary Card (#1) and the Bias Fingerprint (first main module), insert a fixed-position "Attention Zone" slot. This slot always lives at the top of the module stack. Its content is fully dynamic: 1–3 compact two-line action cards sourced from any module. Selection priority: (1) new contradictions since last visit, (2) overdue open loops, (3) newly-confirmed bias pattern, (4) significant Independence Score movement, (5) avoidance alerts. Each card links down to the relevant module. When nothing is urgent or new, the zone is absent — no empty box, just the main modules starting immediately after the Summary Card.

This gives you: always-visible urgent actions in a known slot (the Attention Zone), a stable page layout the user navigates by memory (the section stack), and a fast way to jump to anything (the sticky nav). These three together replace what full dynamic reordering was trying to accomplish — without the cognitive cost.

What decision-context-aware relevance should look like: After a session completes with a specific rule signal (R4 Regret Asymmetry, R5 False Urgency, R1 REDIRECT), the relevant Mirror module gets soft visual prominence: a gold pulse-border on load and a dot badge on its sticky nav label. The module stays in its position — it just signals "this is especially relevant to your latest decision." The Attention Zone also gets a contextual card for it. This is item #20 in the matrix. It is "what is best suited to the decision-maker" answered correctly — not as a layout change, but as a relevance signal on top of a stable structure.
