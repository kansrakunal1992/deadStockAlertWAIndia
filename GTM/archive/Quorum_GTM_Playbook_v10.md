## §2 What is Mirror / Quorum

**Mirror** is an AI-powered personal decision support tool. When a user brings a major life, career, or strategic decision, a council of five distinct AI advisors responds — each from a different cognitive and philosophical perspective.

**The five council members:**

- **Elder** — wisdom, long view, values, what will matter in 10 years
- **Mirror** — reflects what the user is already feeling and weighing, without judgment
- **Scientist** — data, evidence, probabilities, what the research says
- **Strategist** — options, trade-offs, sequencing, second-order effects
- **Examiner** — challenges assumptions, stress-tests the reasoning, finds the blind spot

> **✅ UPDATED — V10:** The Examiner now fires on every session without exception. A proper rule engine (rules R1–R12) detects upstream blockers, irreversibility, missing information, value conflicts, time pressure, and multi-party dynamics. E0 (emotional/gut question) always fires regardless. C0 (JTBD context grounding) always fires. The Examiner-doesn't-always-fire note from V9 is now obsolete. This directly addresses Gagan's Session 11 backlog item and the left-brain critique from Sujitesh (KL #20).

**Mirror** = the personal daily journaling / decision reflection layer. Separate from the council session but deeply integrated.

> **DO NOT:** Explain the product before running it live. Do not pitch features. Do not describe the advisors in the abstract. Run a real decision live, then let the user discover what happened.

## §11 Key Learnings (1–24)

Learnings 1–22 carried forward from V9. Learnings 23–24 new in V10.

1. **Run it live, never explain it first.** The product creates conviction through experience, not description. Any explanation before a live session reduces impact.
2. **Lead with their decision, not the product.** Senior users engage faster when you make the session about something real they are sitting with, not a product showcase.
3. **The session is the product.** The 30-minute live council run is not a demo or a sales step. It is the product itself. Treat it accordingly.
4. **Silence after a strong message is not rejection.** Especially for senior, introspective people. The thinking takes longer than the typing. Wait.
5. **The paid ask belongs at the end of the session.** Not in a follow-up. Not in a separate call. At the natural close of the live experience, before the energy dissipates.
6. **Never make a financial ask in a follow-up message.** A follow-up ask is easier to decline than one made in the presence of the product experience.
7. **The right ICP person knows immediately.** Either they lean in or they do not. If they lean in and still do not convert, the problem is the close mechanics, not conviction.
8. **Group posts are weaker than direct outreach.** Response rates from direct warm messages are 3–5x higher than group posts, even within high-trust alumni groups.
9. **Referrals come from session experience, not awareness.** People who have heard about Mirror do not refer. People who have run a decision through it do.
10. **The 4th follow-up rule.** Never send a fourth follow-up message in a single cycle to any lead. Three messages maximum before parking and resetting the window.
11. **Reactivation works best with a genuine new hook.** "Following up" is not a hook. A product milestone, a first paying user, or a named use case is a hook.
12. **Price resistance is conviction resistance.** If they push back on ₹999, the problem is not the price. The session did not fully land. Do not negotiate.
13. **Never share the link before the session.** Self-exploration without context creates a weak first impression. The session creates the context for self-exploration.
14. **Unprompted sharing is the strongest signal.** "I will share this" is a weak signal. Sharing without being asked is the strongest possible signal (see Viral).
15. **The Mirror frame outperforms the AI council frame.** "Shows you what you are already weighing" is more compelling than "AI council of five advisors." Lead with the output, not the mechanism.
16. **Staged commitments are not nos.** "Let me think," "not now," or "come back in a month" has a different trajectory than "not interested." Treat them differently.
17. **Gift access is for connectors, not prospects.** Gifting Mirror to a pipeline lead removes the friction that makes the conversion meaningful. Reserve gift access for Dora-type connectors.
18. **Change the entry point, not the volume.** If an ask has not worked, the right response is a different entry point — not a louder or softer version of the same ask. (Source: Dhruv pattern.)
19. **Advocates do not start as enthusiasts.** Viral was parked. Gagan was a late-pipeline session. The advocates emerged after sustained low-pressure presence, not during the pitch.
20. **Left-brain partial-surface critique.** When a user engages with only part of the product (the structured council output, but not Mirror or Elder deeply), they may draw conclusions about the whole from a partial experience. Response: address the gap in their experience, not the critique of the product. NOTE V10: The Examiner always fires now — this specific product gap is resolved. (Source: Sujitesh Jun 25.)
21. **Organic advocate arc — do not convert back.** When someone pivots from prospect to organic advocate without being asked — daily use, sharing with founder friends — do not re-pitch them as a customer. Let the channel run on its own terms. The referral value from Viral exceeds the subscription value. (Source: Viral Jun 23.)
22. **Three-messages-in-a-day trap.** When a strong message ends with an open question, the only valid next message is their answer. Sending additional messages before they respond communicates anxiety and neutralises the original close. The 8:48 AM reply to Sujitesh was correct. The 9:00 AM and 6:09 PM messages were not. (Source: Sujitesh Jun 25.)
23. **Real decisions don't arise daily — and that is normal.** DB check Jun 28 confirms Gagan and Viral are not using Mirror daily. This is expected — major decisions do not arise every day for most people. The daily WhatsApp is not intended to drive daily sessions. It maintains presence so that when a real decision does arise, Quorum is the first move. Do not adjust the cadence based on session frequency.
